Ordering at the table

A guest scans the QR on their table, joins with the code their server gives them, and orders in Thai or English. The kitchen sees it immediately. Staff run the floor, the bills and the menu from the same system.

The working system, and photographs of it

Every screen below was captured from the working application, but this page is only pictures of it. To use the real thing — scan a table card with your own phone, send an order, watch it appear on the kitchen board — GitHub will start a copy in the cloud and give you an address any phone can open, on any network. It takes about three minutes and costs nothing.

Thai first, English on one tap. Prime rib is never added like a normal dish: the guest asks staff to weigh it, and only their confirmation of the quoted weight and price creates the order.

No. 02

Behind the pass

Kitchen, floor and cashier

Built for a Friday night: big numbers, one clear action per ticket, and anything that needs attention stated in words as well as colour.

No. 03

What the owner sees

Counted honestly

Every figure says what it counts. Devices are called devices, not people. A dish that was sold out all week is marked as such instead of being called unpopular, and a day with no data looks different from a day with none sold.

What these screens are

  • Captured from the working system, not mocked up. Guest screens are Thai at phone width; staff screens are English on a desktop.
  • The restaurant, dishes, prices and photographs are Rabbit Grill's own, taken from their printed menus. Nothing has been approved by the restaurant yet, so every screen is marked as demo data and the system refuses to take real orders for unverified dishes.
  • Orders, tables, staff and history are generated demo data: a seeded year of trading so the charts have something to show.
  • Everything has been tested on one laptop. No phone on a restaurant network, no hosting and no payment provider has been exercised yet.
